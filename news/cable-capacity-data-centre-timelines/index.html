Cable Capacity Is Another Constraint on Data Center Timelines

Nexans' warning shifts attention from connection offers to the equipment required for energization.

Adapted from the Telegram editorial archive dated 2026-10-26. English edition published 2026-10-26.

The reported warning

Reuters reported on 22 October that Nexans' finance chief described cable manufacturing capacity as a constraint on AI data center construction. The report cited roughly three years to establish a new XLPE cable production line. That is a manufacturing expansion lead time, not a universal delivery time for every cable order.

Beyond the connection offer

Nexans also discusses this issue in its own industry commentary: a grid connection offer is not a commissioned electrical installation. Cable, transformers, switchgear, protection systems, testing, and installation must all align with the energization schedule. Supplier commentary is useful evidence of its perspective, not an independent forecast of every project's outcome.

What developers should check

Confirm specifications, production slots, delivery obligations, installation capacity, testing requirements, and interfaces between contractors. A procurement schedule should identify which components can actually determine the launch date.

Keep liability project-specific

Delay penalties and termination rights depend on signed contracts, caps, exclusions, and responsibility for the delay. There is no universal daily penalty that can be applied to all data center projects. The unsupported penalty examples in the original draft are not repeated here.

Takeaway

Time-to-Power includes the physical supply chain after the connection agreement. A credible schedule links utility milestones to equipment procurement, construction, and commissioning.
