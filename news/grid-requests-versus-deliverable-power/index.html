Grid Requests Are Not Deliverable Power

Why a connection application is only the beginning of data center infrastructure due diligence.

Adapted from the Telegram editorial archive dated 2026-10-22. English edition published 24 October 2026.

The queue is a pipeline, not a power supply

A data center connection request expresses an intention to consume electricity. It does not, by itself, establish that a project controls its site, has secured a customer, can fund network upgrades, or will receive power on its proposed schedule.

What the reporting shows

Reporting by TechRadar, drawing on Wood Mackenzie and The Wall Street Journal, describes widespread duplication and uncertainty in US data center connection requests. It also describes utilities requiring collateral and stronger contractual commitments. These are reported pipeline estimates, not measurements of operating capacity. A low-confidence request should not automatically be classified as fraudulent or commercially worthless.

What an investor should verify

The useful question is not simply whether an application exists. Review site control, the utility study stage, required network upgrades, deposits and security obligations, easements, permits, and the conditions governing the proposed energization date. Distinguish indicative correspondence from executed agreements.

The trade-off

Financial security can discourage speculative applications and reduce the risk that other customers pay for unused infrastructure. It can also raise barriers for smaller developers with credible projects. Screening is not a substitute for examining each project.

Takeaway

A nearby substation and a queue position are starting points for investigation, not proof of deliverable power. Land valuation should reflect the evidence supporting connection, its cost, and its timing.
